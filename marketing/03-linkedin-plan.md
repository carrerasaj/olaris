### 2. Industry Commentary
Reacting to fleet industry news, trends, and current events sourced from Google Alerts. Position yourself as responding thoughtfully, not just amplifying.

**Google Alerts Hook Pattern:**
"[News headline/topic] dropped recently. Here's what actually matters to fleet operators..."

**Example 1:**
"Just saw another article about fuel prices. What I never see mentioned: aggressive driving can add 8p per mile in fuel costs. That's not a market variable—that's controllable. Yet most operators treat fuel as something that happens to them rather than something they can actively manage. Real driver behaviour monitoring should reduce fuel spend by 8-15%. That's not hypothetical—that's what we see. But you have to measure it."

**Example 2:**
"Licence checking in the news again. Good. It should be. But here's the gap: DVLA checks happen after you've already hired or deployed a driver. Real fleet intelligence should flag licence issues, CPC compliance drift, and medical issues before they become your problem. That's not bureaucracy—that's liability protection and driver safety. Your insurance company should demand it."

**Example 3:**
"Reading about lease company acquisition activity. Never slows down. What does change: the operators that get acquired are the ones with clean data, happy drivers, controlled costs. The chaos—bad driver behaviour, hidden mileage overages, surprise expenses—those ops get picked up for less or don't get picked up at all. Data integrity matters more than most people admit."

## Google Alerts to LinkedIn Post: 10-Minute Template

### Step 1: Read the Alert (2 min)
You get an email from Google Alerts about fleet industry news. Read it.

### Step 2: Extract the Insight (2 min)
Don't just summarise the article. Ask:
- What does this mean for fleet operators?
- What problem does this highlight?
- What's the gap between the news and the reality I see in fleets?
- What would I tell a fleet manager about this?

### Step 3: Write Your Take (4 min)
Structure:
1. **Hook:** Reference the news briefly without just retelling it
2. **Insight:** Your specific perspective (the thing a marketing article wouldn't say)
3. **Implication:** What operators should actually do about it
4. **CTA:** Question or soft call to conversation

### Step 4: Add Hashtags (1 min)
- 5-6 hashtags
- Mix popular (#FleetManagement, #OperationsLeadership) with niche (#FleetCost, #DriverBehaviour)

### Step 5: Post (1 min)
Done.

## Comment Strategy: Building Visibility Without Posting

Every day, spend 5-10 minutes engaging on others' posts. You don't have to post 5x/week if you're commenting thoughtfully 3-4 times daily.

**Where to find posts:**
- LinkedIn feed (follow fleet-related pages: Institute of Fleet Management, Logistics UK, etc.)
- Search hashtags: #FleetManagement, #OperationsLeadership, #SupplyChain
- Follow 10-15 fleet operators' personal profiles and comment on their posts

**How to comment (not just like):**
- Answer a question they posed
- Build on their point with a specific insight
- Disagree thoughtfully (this gets engagement)
- Ask a question that sparks conversation

**Comment template:**
"Great point on [topic]. In my experience, the gap is usually [specific thing]. We've seen [brief example]. What's your take on [related question]?"

## Post Timing for UK B2B Fleet Audience

### Best Times to Post
- **Weekdays only** (Mon-Fri)
- **8-9am GMT:** Commute scroll, high engagement
- **12-1pm GMT:** Lunch break, second peak
- **4-5pm GMT:** End of workday, wrap-up check

### Avoid
- Early morning (before 7am) — not active yet
- After 6pm — they've left the office
- Weekends — minimal B2B engagement
- Friday afternoon — people are checked out

### Why This Works
Fleet managers are operational people. They check LinkedIn during transitions (commute, lunch). Posting during deep work time (10-12, 2-4) is wasted.
