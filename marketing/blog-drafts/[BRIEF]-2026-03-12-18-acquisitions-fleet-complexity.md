## Brief

Over 12 years at Bridle Group (now Jurni Leasing), Alan personally sourced, negotiated, carried out due diligence on, and led the integration of 18 acquisitions — some seven-figure deals. This post draws on that experience to explain why fleet operations become exponentially more complex with growth, and why fleet intelligence tools exist to solve that complexity.

## Key Points to Cover

1. **The pattern across 18 deals** — every acquired company had different systems, different processes, different ways of tracking the same data. Day 1 after acquisition was always about untangling the mess.

2. **The hidden cost of integration** — it's never just "add more vehicles to the spreadsheet." Each acquisition brought different funder relationships, different SLAs, different KPIs, different reporting formats. Multiply that 18 times.

3. **The tribal knowledge problem** — acquired companies often had one person who "knew everything." When they left, critical operational knowledge walked out the door. No system, no documentation, no continuity.

4. **Why spreadsheets break at scale** — what works for 500 vehicles doesn't work for 5,000. And what works for 5,000 with one funder doesn't work for 37,000 across dozens of funders.

5. **The moment I realised we needed something better** — the catalyst for building Olaris. Frame the transition from "I wish this existed" to "we're building it."

6. **What fleet intelligence actually solves** — a single view of truth across all vehicles, all funders, all contracts. The thing that would have saved months of integration work on every single acquisition.
