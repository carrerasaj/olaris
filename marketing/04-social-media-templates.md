## Google Alerts to LinkedIn Post Template

### Overview
You receive 10 fleet industry emails daily from Google Alerts. Most fleet managers let them pile up. Instead, convert 2-3 per week into LinkedIn posts.

This template takes 10 minutes.

---

### Step 1: Read the Alert (2 minutes)
Email lands. Read the headline and article summary.

**Common alert topics:**
- Fuel price changes
- Insurance claims / safety data releases
- Compliance updates (DVLA, CPC, medical fitness)
- EV adoption trends
- Fleet industry M&A news
- Logistics / supply chain challenges
- Driver shortage commentary
- Vehicle recall news
- Government transport policy

---

### Step 2: Ask Yourself (2 minutes)
Don't just rewrite the article. You have an unfair advantage: 40 years of fleet operations experience.

Ask:
- What does this headline actually mean for fleet operators?
- What problem is this highlighting?
- What's the gap between what the article says and what I know about real fleet operations?
- What would I tell a fleet manager about this if we were having coffee?
- Is there a contrarian take here? (Often the most interesting LinkedIn posts disagree with the obvious interpretation.)

**Write a one-line insight.** This becomes your post hook.

---

### Step 3: Outline Your Post (2 minutes)

**Structure:**
1. **Hook** (1-2 sentences): Reference the news without just retelling it
2. **Insight** (2-3 sentences): Your specific perspective—the thing a marketing article wouldn't say
3. **Implication** (1-2 sentences): What operators should actually understand or do about this
4. **CTA** (1 sentence): Question or invitation to conversation

**Example outline (from fuel price alert):**
- Hook: "Fuel costs in the news again"
- Insight: "But the real story is driver behaviour impact on fuel, which is actually controllable"
- Implication: "Most operators don't measure this, so they're missing millions in preventable spend"
- CTA: "What's your biggest controllable cost right now?"

---

### Step 4: Write Your Post (2 minutes)

Use the examples from the "20 Ready-to-Post" section above as tone / structure templates.

**Tone:** Insider, specific, honest. You're the fleet director at a conference, not a marketing department.

**Length:** 150-300 words.

**Formula:**
- Hook (1-2 sentences) = Gets attention
- Insight (2-3 paragraphs) = Shows expertise
- Implication (1-2 sentences) = Makes it actionable
- CTA (1 sentence) = Invites engagement

---

### Step 5: Add Hashtags (1 minute)

**Structure:** 3 primary + 2-3 niche

**Primary hashtags (always include):**
- #FleetManagement (broad, high engagement)
- #OperationsLeadership (decision-makers)
- One topic-specific: #EV, #Compliance, #DriverBehaviour, etc.

**Niche hashtags (rotate 2-3):**

- #DriverBehaviour
- #FleetCompliance

### Step 6: Post (1 minute)

## Engagement Comment Templates

**Where to find posts to comment on:**
- LinkedIn feed (follow fleet, logistics, supply chain pages)
- Search hashtags: #FleetManagement #OperationsLeadership #Logistics

**Rule:** Comment thoughtfully. No generic "Great post!" Comments that answer a question, build on a point, or respectfully disagree get engagement.

**When they ask:** "How do you manage excess mileage in your fleet?"

### Comment Template 3: Respectful Disagreement
**When they claim:** "Driver behaviour monitoring is invasive and kills morale"

**Template:**
"Interesting perspective. I'd push back slightly though—it depends entirely on how it's framed and used. Monitoring for coaching and safety is different from monitoring for punishment. We've seen morale actually improve when drivers know the system is fair and designed to help them, not catch them out. What's your experience been?"

**Why it works:** Respectfully disagrees, provides nuance, doesn't sound defensive, asks for their experience.

"Had something similar years ago. Turned out to be one driver's aggressive braking and acceleration pattern on a specific route. When we had visibility into the driver behaviour, not just the fuel outcome, we could actually fix it. Took two weeks of coaching. Did you manage to trace yours back to a specific cause, or still investigating?"

**Why it works:** Shares relevant experience, shows empathy, provides specific example, asks a follow-up.

"This is crucial territory. Are you mostly concerned about infrastructure readiness, total cost of ownership modelling, driver training, or something else? The EV challenges for fleet operators are so varied depending on your specific operation. What's the biggest blocker you're hitting?"

**Why it works:** Shows you understand it's complex, asks specifically, invites them to be detailed.

### Comment Template 6: Offering Perspective from Your Space
**When they ask:** "How do you think the driver shortage affects fleet operations?"

"Shortage aside, I think the real issue is that fleets aren't competitive on driver experience. If you can't show a driver you're tracking their behaviour fairly, coaching them, and rewarding good performance, you're fighting uphill against better employers. The operators winning on driver retention right now are the ones who've made driver engagement a priority, not an afterthought. That's a data and systems problem more than a shortage problem."

**Why it works:** Offers contrarian insight, shows expertise, slightly provocative (gets engagement).

**Why it works:** Validates their point, shows you have a solution perspective, asks how they're currently doing it.

**Why it works:** Provides specific data point, shows results are possible, asks their approach.

**Why it works:** Validates frustration, offers insight, implies there's a solution, asks diagnostic question.

## Engagement Strategy

### Daily Commitment (5-10 minutes)
- Check LinkedIn feed first thing in morning (during commute, 8-9am)
- Find 1-2 relevant posts from fleet operators or logistics leaders
- Comment thoughtfully using templates above
- Spend 5 minutes in evening checking replies to your posts

### Weekly Commitment
- 1-2 substantive comments on other people's posts daily = 7-14 engagements
- Comment on your own posts if others are engaging
- Follow 2-3 new fleet operators or logistics leaders (build list)

### Result
- Posts get more visibility (comments push your posts higher in feed)
- You get visibility from commenting (people see your comments on others' posts)
- Builds relationships with other operators and decision-makers
- Over time, creates reputation as thoughtful, credible voice in fleet space
